Case study

The Valerie and Michael Miller Music Therapy Studio | ADI Negev–Nahalat Eran

From an existing space to a calibrated music therapy studio that therapists run on their own. April–July 2026.

Arrange a visitDownload the case study (PDF)

Key facts

Client (contracting party)
ADI Negev–Nahalat Eran hospital, part of a rehabilitation village that includes a rehabilitation hospital, a special education school and residences for people with disabilities
Funding
A dedicated gift. The studio is named for Valerie and Michael Miller
Who uses the room
Music therapists, working with residents, special education students and rehabilitation patients
Timeline
About four months (April–July 2026). Opened in August 2026
Scope
Full project: infrastructure renovation (floors, ceilings, electrical and lighting), acoustic treatment, audio systems, instruments, smart lighting, tablet control and a Dolby Atmos listening station

Challenge and solution

The challenge

Turn an existing space into a room that serves several populations, each needing a different room setup, and that staff can run without a technician.

The solution

Acoustic design and infrastructure from scratch; instruments chosen for a hospital setting (for example, quieter electronic drums, since the room is close to the entrance); and a tablet interface with saved scenes. A wall remote at the door turns the room on and switches between modes: play and therapy, or listening.

Why it works

Therapists switch the room to the setup each session needs, with no engineer on call. The room can grow in modules as the program grows.

To the best of my knowledge, this is the first music therapy room in Israel and the Middle East with a full Dolby Atmos listening station.
