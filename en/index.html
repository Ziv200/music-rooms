Music therapy rooms for institutions in Israel

A music therapy room your donors can name, and your therapists can run on their own

I design and build music therapy rooms for rehabilitation centers, hospitals and care institutions in Israel, end to end: acoustic survey and 3D planning, infrastructure and cabling, acoustic treatment and speakers, and control-software integration and calibration. Staff run the room from a tablet with saved scenes, with no sound engineer needed for each session.

Latest project: The Valerie and Michael Miller Music Therapy Studio at ADI Negev–Nahalat Eran, opened in August 2026.

How we work together

  1. 1

    Free needs assessment: who will use the room, for which sessions, and in which space. In most projects it ends with an initial room program.

  2. 2

    Acoustic survey and measurements on site.

  3. 3

    3D design, specification and estimate, plus a donor brief if needed.

  4. 4

    Infrastructure: signal, power, network and, where needed, construction work, coordinated with the institution's engineering team.

  5. 5

    Acoustic treatment, speakers and instruments.

  6. 6

    Integration and calibration: control software, scenes for each group, and testing.

  7. 7

    Handover and training: a calibrated room that staff run from a tablet, staff training, and ongoing support and scene updates after handover.

In the sample project, the work from the space as received to a calibrated room took about four months (April–July 2026). Every project's timeline depends on its scope.

About me

I'm Ilan Ziv, 32: a musician, sound engineer and software developer, and a graduate of the Jerusalem Academy of Music and Dance. For years I performed with leading Israeli artists, and later worked with them as a sound engineer. Today I design music rooms and develop audio software.

That combination is rare: I understand a room as a player, as an acoustics and systems engineer, and as the developer of the software that runs it. So I can take a project from empty space to a calibrated room that staff run themselves.

Music therapy is also personal for me: two of my siblings have special needs, and I know firsthand how much a place to play, listen and express yourself matters.

FAQ

Who runs the room day to day?

+

The staff. The room is controlled from a tablet and a wall remote, with saved scenes for each type of session. No sound engineer is needed for each session.

What does it cost?

+

Every room is priced after a needs assessment and a site survey, based on the space, infrastructure and modules. There is a full project and a smaller Core Room in an existing space. The initial needs assessment is free of charge.

Can we start small and expand?

+

Yes. Rooms are modular: start with a Core Room and add instruments, smart lighting, recording or a Dolby Atmos listening station later.

How are these rooms funded?

+

Many institutions fund a music room through a dedicated gift, as in the sample project. I can prepare a one-page project brief for the development office to share with donors.

What happens after handover?

+

Staff receive structured training at handover, and I remain available for support, questions and scene updates. Service terms are set in each project agreement.

Can we see a room like this?

+

Yes. Visits to the studio I built in the sample project can be arranged when no therapy session is in progress, coordinated in advance through me.
